z486 is z386's successor: a pipelined front end, common instructions in hardware, and an x87. Around it sits the AT board again, now with a Tseng ET4000, a Sound Blaster 16, plug and play cards and a CD-ROM drive, and it runs Windows 95.

## What we ran on it

- MS-DOS 6.22, with 256-colour demos such as Juice
- Windows for Workgroups 3.11 in 386 enhanced mode
- Windows 95 OSR2, which finds the Sound Blaster and the network card by itself

Ours: the ET4000 (from Tseng's databook, with 86Box as a second opinion), the Sound Blaster 16, the NE2000, the plug and play logic and the second-level cache. The IDE drives are software, in Linux.

## What we changed in z486

z486 is shared with the Pentium-class machine. Anything that is Pentium-only sits behind a parameter, so the 486 stays a 486. What it got are mostly bug fixes:

- faults during fault delivery shut the CPU down, instead of looping
- an IRET to an outer privilege level can be restarted after a fault, as Windows' virtual machine manager expects
- a pending interrupt isn't lost when another one arrives during its acknowledge
- x87 stores that fault keep the status word, and several x87 corner cases
- a cache fill that a store races doesn't install stale code
